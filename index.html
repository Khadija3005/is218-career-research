04 / Research becomes design

Two brand and layout directions

A · Clear systems — selected

Ink, muted teal and warm white. Serif headings, readable body text, clear sections and evidence tables.

Message: organized, thoughtful and able to explain decisions. Recruiters reach project evidence quickly.

Tradeoff: less playful; needs specific project stories to feel personal.

B · Creative project journal

Warm brown and peach, image-led project tiles and a narrative timeline.

Message: approachable and creative, with more emphasis on the project journey.

Tradeoff: screenshots and long stories could push verification links farther down the page.

Decision: use A for the research site and as the leading portfolio hypothesis. The reviewer should compare whether A or B better communicates the same project evidence. These are visual directions; two finished portfolios are not required.

Small portfolio specification

Planned structure: introduction → individual case study → group contribution → professional materials. Every case study will show the problem, my contribution, a decision, a checked result and an honest limit. Link existing issues, changes and checks instead of duplicating reports.

Current evidence: the Café Rush plan documents bounded stories and proposed checks. It is planning evidence, not a working game or production AI experience. A group case study will be added only when the actual contribution and shared repository can be linked.

Later stage: review résumé contact details before publication; add owned LinkedIn and professional contact links when ready. Résumé publication and a finished portfolio are outside this Plan review.

05 / Five short stories

What the reader should be able to do

  1. As an internship recruiter, I want to identify my professional direction quickly so I can judge relevance. Check: a partner explains the goal after reading the introduction.
  2. As a reviewer, I want original sources and checked dates so I can inspect the research. Check: open all three employer links and compare the requirements.
  3. As a hiring manager, I want to distinguish my actual contribution from planned work. Check: case studies label personal contribution, evidence and limitations.
  4. As a reader, I want to move between career and programming research. Check: follow the link in each direction.
  5. As a phone or keyboard user, I want readable content and clear navigation. Check: inspect a narrow screen, tab through links and use the skip link.

Next step: present the site to a classmate, get specific feedback, then update this same record. By November 19, 2026, aim to outline one workflow case study with an API contract and three acceptance checks; this is a proposed learning milestone.

Partner task and resolution workflow
  1. Find the question, compare the two directions and identify the working choice without coaching.
  2. Open one employer page and compare a requirement with the site.
  3. Compare the two design options and explain one concrete improvement.
  4. Post the page/section, expected versus observed behavior and a specific suggestion in the issue.
  5. The owner records accept/investigate/defer/decline with a reason, links any change and verification, then asks the reviewer to retry.

Leave the issue open until the reviewer records a successful retry for resolved feedback. No reviewer identity or feedback has been invented.
